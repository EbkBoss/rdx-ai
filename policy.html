What is not permitted

On this site

  • Automated crawling and scraping — including "just fetching the HTML", headless browsers, search-and-extract agents, price harvesters and mirror sites.
  • AI training and retrieval harvesting — including feeding the page, its copy, its prices, its proof images or its metadata into a training set, an embedding index, a knowledge base, an agent memory or an LLM context.
  • Copying and mirroring — the copy, the layout, the proof images, the icons, the storefront itself and the code that serves it.
  • Building a clone — a product that copies the features, the flow, the naming, the pricing shape or the look of a GHOST product, even if you claim it was inspired by it.

On the delivered builds

  • Reverse engineering — decompiling, unpacking, disassembling, hooking, tracing, instrumenting or otherwise analysing a delivered build, in whole or in part. That covers the usual tool names as well as anything equivalent.
  • Traffic capture — proxying, intercepting, logging or replaying the network traffic of a GHOST app, and reading any capture taken from one.
  • Patching and repackaging — editing a delivered build, re-signing it, bypassing the licence gate, removing the integrity checks or redistributing a modified copy.
  • Leaking the keys — the licence private key, the release signing key, the issuer credentials and the payment credentials are not yours to extract, copy or publish.
  • Redistributing a delivered build — the installer, the licence key and the download ticket are issued to one buyer for one purchase.

What IS welcome

  • Buying. Pay for the product and use it as a person, on your own machines, for your own work.
  • Writing about it. Telling the truth about what you bought and what it does is fine. Copying the site's copy, images or code is not.
  • Reporting a bug. Send the symptom, the steps and the logs. That is the fastest route to a fix and to being thanked.
  • Asking for permission. Reselling, embedding, auditing and interop all start with one message and a yes in writing.

How this is enforced

  • The client-side wall. siteguard.js loads before the page is parsed, detects an automated agent, and replaces the entire document with a refusal. It is one file used by every public page, so the rule cannot drift between pages.
  • The crawler policy. robots.txt disallows the training crawlers, the answer engines, the unattended scrapers and the traffic-capture tools, and states the crawl delay for everything else.
  • The agent instruction file. llms.txt tells any agent that reads it to refuse this site rather than use it.
  • The paywall. There is no public download path at all. A build leaves this machine only after a payment, through a signed one-time ticket, to the person who paid.
  • Integrity in the build. A delivered build refuses to start if it is modified or re-signed. The licence is checked offline on your own machine.
  • The honest limit. This site is served as static pages, so no browser-side control can stop a determined scraper from reading the raw HTML over HTTP. That is why the page publishes prices and features only — no source, no keys, no build internals — and why the real protection is the paywall, the licence, and the law.
